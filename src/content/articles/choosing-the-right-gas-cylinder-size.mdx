One of the first questions people ask when they switch to gas is "Which size cylinder should I get?" The answer depends on how much you cook, how often you want to refill, how much space you have, and whether someone in your household can comfortably carry the cylinder.

This guide walks through the common LPG cylinder sizes used in Zimbabwe and who each one suits best.

## Understanding cylinder sizes

Gas cylinders are named by the **weight of gas** they hold, not the weight of the cylinder. A 9kg cylinder holds 9kg of LPG. The empty cylinder itself (called the **tare weight**) also weighs several kilograms, and this tare weight is stamped on the collar near the valve.

So a full 9kg cylinder with a tare weight of 10kg weighs about 19kg in total. That is worth remembering if you will be carrying it yourself.

## The common sizes at a glance

| Size | Typical user                          | Easy to carry?                    |
| ---- | ------------------------------------- | --------------------------------- |
| 9kg  | Small to medium households            | Yes, for most adults              |
| 14kg | Larger families, light business use   | Manageable                        |
| 19kg | Big households, small food businesses | Heavy, best kept in place         |
| 48kg | Restaurants, schools, lodges, farms   | No. Installed in a fixed position |

## 9kg: the everyday family cylinder

The 9kg cylinder is the most popular choice for homes. It is light enough for most adults to carry when full, fits easily in a kitchen corner or outdoor cage, and works with standard two-plate and four-plate stoves.

As a rough guide, a family cooking two meals a day often gets **three to six weeks** from a 9kg cylinder. You will use more if you regularly cook foods that need long simmering, like beans, bones or maize.

**Best for:** couples and families of up to five or six people who cook mostly on gas.

## 14kg: fewer trips to refill

A 14kg cylinder holds over 50% more gas than a 9kg, so you refill less often. It is a good step up for larger households, or for homes that also use gas for a heater or a small water heater.

It is heavier to move, so it suits homes where the cylinder can stay in one place, ideally outside with a hose through the wall.

**Best for:** larger families, homes cooking every meal on gas, and small home businesses.

## 19kg: for big households and small businesses

The 19kg cylinder is popular with big households, home bakeries, small takeaways and tuck shops. It lasts much longer between refills, which saves time.

At this size, the cylinder should be installed in a fixed, ventilated position. It is not something to carry in and out of the kitchen every day.

**Best for:** large or extended families, small food businesses, offices and staff kitchens.

## 48kg: commercial and institutional kitchens

48kg cylinders are designed for high-volume cooking. Restaurants, takeaways, boarding schools, hospitals, lodges and farms usually use one or more 48kg cylinders connected through a manifold, so that one cylinder can be swapped while another keeps supplying gas.

These setups should always be planned and installed by a trained technician, with cylinders kept outside in a secure, ventilated cage.

**Best for:** restaurants, takeaways, schools and institutions, hotels and lodges, farms using gas brooders.

## Questions to ask yourself

Before you choose, think about:

1. **How many people do you cook for, and how often?** More meals means more gas.
2. **What do you cook?** Long-simmered foods use a lot more gas than quick stir-fries.
3. **Who will move the cylinder?** If it needs to be carried often, a 9kg is usually best.
4. **Where will it be kept?** Larger cylinders need a safe, ventilated, permanent spot, ideally outdoors.
5. **Do you use gas for anything else?** Heaters, water heaters and brooders all add to your usage.
6. **How often do you want to refill?** A bigger cylinder means fewer trips.

## A note on having a spare

Many households keep a second cylinder so they never run out halfway through cooking. If you do, store the spare **outside**, upright, in a shaded and ventilated spot, with the valve closed. Never keep spare cylinders in bedrooms, cupboards or basements. Our [gas safety guide](/gas-safety#storage) explains safe storage in more detail.
