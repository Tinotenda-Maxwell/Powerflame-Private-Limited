Checking your gas setup for leaks is one of the most useful safety habits you can learn. It takes about two minutes, costs nothing, and you only need washing-up liquid and water. You should do it every time you connect a new or refilled cylinder, and once a month as a routine check.

<Callout title="If you can smell gas right now" tone="warning">
  Do not do a leak test. Do not switch anything on or off. Close the cylinder valve if it is safe to
  reach, open doors and windows, get everyone outside, and call for help from outside. See [what to
  do if you smell gas](/gas-safety#smell-gas).
</Callout>

## Signs that you might have a gas leak

Gas leaks are usually small and slow, but there are clear warning signs:

- **A smell like rotten eggs or cabbage** near the cylinder or stove when everything is turned off. LPG has an odorant added so leaks can be noticed.
- **A hissing sound** near the cylinder valve, regulator or hose.
- **Your gas running out much faster than usual**, even though you are cooking the same way.
- **A gas leak detector alarm** going off.
- **A frosty patch** on the cylinder, valve or hose that is not near the burner.

If you notice any of these, carry out the check below or ask a technician to do it for you.

## What you need

- A small bowl or cup
- Washing-up liquid (dishwashing soap)
- Water
- A small paintbrush, sponge or spray bottle

Mix about one part washing-up liquid to three parts water. You want a solution that bubbles easily.

## Step-by-step: the soapy water test

### 1. Make sure the area is safe

Check that there are no flames nearby. Turn off all the burners on your stove and do not smoke. Open a window or door for ventilation.

### 2. Open the cylinder valve

With the stove knobs all closed, open the cylinder valve. This puts gas pressure into the regulator and hose without letting it out at the burner.

### 3. Apply the soapy water

Brush, sponge or spray the solution generously onto each place where gas could escape:

- The cylinder valve and the area around its spindle
- Where the regulator connects to the cylinder
- Where the hose connects to the regulator
- Along the full length of the hose, especially at bends
- Where the hose connects to the stove, and the clamps

### 4. Watch closely

Look carefully at each point for about 30 seconds. If you see bubbles growing or foaming, gas is escaping at that point. A small leak might produce only a slow cluster of tiny bubbles, so take your time.

### 5. Close the valve and wipe clean

When you have finished, close the cylinder valve and wipe off the soapy water with a damp cloth.

<Callout title="Never use a flame">
  Never use a match, lighter or candle to look for a gas leak. It is dangerous and could cause a
  fire or explosion. Soapy water is safer and more reliable.
</Callout>

## What to do if you find a leak

1. **Close the cylinder valve** straight away.
2. **Ventilate the area** by opening doors and windows. Remember that LPG is heavier than air and settles near the floor.
3. **Do not use the stove** until the problem is fixed.
4. **Identify the faulty part.** Leaks at the hose usually mean the hose needs replacing. Leaks at a connection may mean a missing or worn washer or a loose clamp. A leak at the cylinder valve itself means the cylinder should go back to your gas supplier.
5. **Replace or repair it properly.** Use only LPG-rated parts. If you are not confident, ask a trained technician.

Do not try to fix a leaking cylinder valve yourself, and never wrap tape around a leaking hose as a "quick fix".

## How to prevent leaks

Most leaks come from worn or poorly fitted parts. You can prevent many of them by following a few simple rules:

- Replace hoses every two to five years, or sooner if they are cracked, stiff or swollen.
- Use the correct regulator for your cylinder and appliance.
- Use proper hose clamps rather than wire or string.
- Keep hoses away from heat, sharp edges and places where they can be stepped on.
- Keep cylinders upright and handle them carefully so valves are not damaged.
