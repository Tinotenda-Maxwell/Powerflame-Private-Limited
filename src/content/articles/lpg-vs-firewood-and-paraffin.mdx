Walk through almost any Harare suburb in the evening and you will see the change happening. Where there used to be wood smoke and paraffin stoves, more and more kitchens now have a gas cylinder in the corner. Load-shedding, the rising cost and scarcity of firewood, and a growing awareness of indoor smoke have all pushed families to look for a better way to cook.

This guide compares LPG (liquefied petroleum gas) with firewood, charcoal and paraffin, so you can decide what makes sense for your home.

## The quick comparison

|                        | LPG gas                  | Firewood / charcoal   | Paraffin                      |
| ---------------------- | ------------------------ | --------------------- | ----------------------------- |
| Lights                 | Instantly                | Slowly                | Fairly quickly                |
| Heat control           | Precise, turn up or down | Hard to control       | Limited                       |
| Smoke indoors          | Very little              | Heavy                 | Moderate, with fumes          |
| Soot on pots and walls | Almost none              | Heavy                 | Some                          |
| Works in power cuts    | Yes                      | Yes                   | Yes                           |
| Storage                | One cylinder             | Bulky, attracts pests | Liquid containers, spill risk |

## Health: the biggest reason to switch

Cooking over firewood or charcoal indoors fills the kitchen with smoke that contains fine particles and carbon monoxide. The World Health Organization links household air pollution from solid fuels and paraffin to serious lung and heart illness, and it is the women and children who spend the most time near the fire who are affected most.

LPG burns cleanly. A properly adjusted gas burner gives a steady blue flame with very little smoke, so the air in your kitchen stays far cleaner. Many families who switch notice the difference within weeks: fewer coughs, no more stinging eyes, and walls and ceilings that stay clean.

## Time and convenience

Firewood has to be bought or collected, stored, kept dry and split. Lighting a fire and waiting for coals can take half an hour before you even start cooking. Paraffin needs to be bought in small amounts, carried home and poured carefully.

With gas, you turn a knob and you are cooking. You can boil water for tea in a few minutes, keep a pot simmering on a low flame, or turn the heat right up for frying. That control means less burnt food and fewer wasted ingredients.

## What about the cost?

This is the question everyone asks. Getting started with gas does cost more upfront, because you need a cylinder, a regulator, a hose and a stove. After that, you only pay for refills.

Whether gas is cheaper than firewood or paraffin month to month depends on the prices in your area and how you cook. But many households find that the real cost of firewood is higher than it looks once you count the time spent collecting or buying it, the soot-damaged pots, and the fact that a wood fire burns long after the cooking is done. Gas only burns while you need it.

A few habits make your gas go further:

- **Use lids on pots.** Food cooks faster and uses less gas.
- **Turn the flame down once food boils.** A gentle simmer uses a fraction of the gas.
- **Match the flame to the pot.** Flames that lick up the sides of a pot are wasted heat.
- **Soak beans overnight** and consider a pressure cooker for long-cooking foods.
- **Keep burners clean** so the flame stays blue and efficient.

## Is gas safe?

LPG is very safe when it is installed and used properly. Most gas accidents are caused by damaged hoses, poor storage or not knowing what to do when there is a leak, and all of these can be avoided.

The basics are simple: keep your cylinder upright and in a ventilated place, use an LPG-rated regulator and hose, check for leaks with soapy water, and close the cylinder valve when you finish cooking. Our [gas safety guide](/gas-safety) covers everything in detail, including exactly what to do if you ever smell gas.

<Callout title="Good to know">
  LPG is heavier than air. If it leaks, it sinks and collects at floor level rather than floating
  away. That is why cylinders should never be kept in basements, pits or near drains.
</Callout>

For most Harare homes, LPG is cleaner, faster and more convenient than firewood, charcoal or paraffin, and it keeps working when the power goes off. With the right equipment and a few good habits, it is also very safe.
